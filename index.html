Highlight

Strategic agreement to develop border protection measures signed!

On Tuesday, May 26, during the Third National Forum on Integrated Border Protection, an event held at the headquarters of the Brazilian Development Bank (BNDES) in Rio de Janeiro, the bank and the Institutional Security Office of the Presidency of the Republic (GSI) signed a remarkable agreement. The agreement is a protocol of intent that aims to create institutional cooperation to stimulate the exchange of knowledge, diagnostics, and technical data between the institutions.

This protocol was designed to support the development of projects in public safety, strategic infrastructure, innovation, monitoring, environmental protection, and strengthening the state’s presence in Brazil’s coastal and maritime regions. According to the president of the BNDES, “The more secure our maritime borders are, greater are the opportunities for investment and business.”

Alongside the emphasis on security, the session's focus was on economic growth. It became clear that marine mining and ocean resources are expected to be the next growth areas for the Brazilian economy. However, this activity must advance in harmony with security of natural ocean resources, sustainability, and respect for biodiversity.

Due to the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and the volatility of oil prices, Brazil’s position as a major offshore producer has become even more strategic. That said, the National Bank is seeking to join forces with Vale and Petrobras to accelerate exploration efforts and position Brazil as a competitive force in the market.

In addition, the president states that the Marine Spatial Planning, developed by the federal government in partnership with the Navy and with financial support from the BNDES, is mapping potential mineral areas that “need to be analyzed more rigorously.” He also argues that Brazil’s “Blue Amazon” must be considered a strategic part of the national territory.

Over the past three years, the BNDES Azul program has already allocated R$ 21.8 billion to logistics, security, and monitoring projects in the sector. The priorities are to expand surveillance at ports, modernize port infrastructure, encourage fish farming, and make Brazil a leader in renewable fuel for shipping.

The other leading figures who attended the meeting emphasized that development and protection go side by side. In this regard, Minister Marcos Antônio Amaro dos Santos believes that protecting the maritime border should be treated as a strategic national priority, since: “It is along the coast that the country’s major flows of foreign trade, energy, data, and critical infrastructure are concentrated.”

Panel discussions were held to address related topics such as opportunities and challenges along the maritime border. The seminar also featured representatives from the Federal Revenue Service and Petrobras, who discussed the impacts of transnational illicit activities, as well as the role of new fronts in energy exploration to ensure the country’s self-sufficiency over the next decade.

Other News

Investments of R$ 2.8 billion in Amazonas.

Petrobras and Transpetro will make investments exceeding R$ 2.8 billion in the state of Amazonas between now and 2030. The country’s president, along with the presidents of Petrobras and Transpetro, attended the announcement ceremony held on Wednesday, May 27, where they detailed how the funds will be allocated. With investments exceeding R$ 60 billion, the project includes FPSOs (P-81 and P-87), 32 wells, and a 134 km gas pipeline, aiming to significantly boost oil and gas output.

The ceremony took place at the Bertolini Construção Naval da Amazônia shipyard. This shipyard will be responsible for building 18 barges ordered by Transpetro. The goal is to achieve greater logistical efficiency in the supply of marine fuel at the country’s ports.

The Mar Aberto Program, responsible for investing in the construction of these barges, plans to build 96 barges by 2030. Transpetro has also contracted 18 pushboats, which will be used for the supply and logistics of marine fuel in Rio de Janeiro (RJ), Santos (SP), Belém (PA), Paranaguá (PR), and Rio Grande (RS).

At the event, they also announced the resumption of Petrobras’ investments into Urucu production, which is the most important oil reserve in Amazonia and the largest onshore oil province in Brazil. The state-owned company will allocate approximately R$ 2.5 billion for drilling new oil wells.

In addition, plans are in place to lay approximately 40 kilometers of pipelines to connect the new oil wells in Urucu, which are expected to boost production by an average of 4,400 barrels per day.

Starting in 2028, the partnership between Petrobras and Amazônica Energy, signed in November of last year, will begin operating and will increase energy security in the northern region of the country by at least 100,000 m³/day.

The Sergipe Deepwater Project (SEAP) opens up a new frontier in oil production.

Petrobras has been making significant investments in the state of Sergipe. In addition to resuming operations at the Sergipe Fertilizer and Nitrogen Plant (Fafen-SE) at the end of last year, on May 29, an investment of an additional R$70 billion in the state was announced.

The amount covers deepwater oil and gas exploration and production, as well as the decommissioning of shallow-water platforms, with more than R$60 billion to be invested in the development of the Sergipe Deepwater (SEAP) project, as outlined in the company’s Strategic Plan.

Oil production is expected to begin in 2030, with gas exports starting in 2031. The project involves the construction of two FPSOs, the drilling and interconnection of 32 wells, and the installation of a gas pipeline approximately 134 km long.

The bidding process for the supply of Wet Christmas Trees (ANMs) and subsea equipment for the two projects is already underway. By the end of the year, bidding for the remaining infrastructure will begin.

In long-term, Petrobras will invest approximately R$12.5 billion in the decommissioning of 26 shallow-water oil platforms in Sergipe by 2035. The process, which began in late 2025, involves plugging 169 wells and removing structures, generating approximately 950 direct jobs and creating demand in the local supply chain.
