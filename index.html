Innovation in Marine Fuel

Shipping companies, including Maersk and the Brazilian mining company Vale, are turning their attention to ethanol as a marine fuel to reduce harmful emissions.

Earlier this month, the Maersk shipping group completed its first voyages using only ethanol, after previous tests with ethanol blends on a vessel designed to run on both methanol and fuel oil.

Ethanol contains about 35% more energy per kilogram than methanol, which means ships need less fuel to travel the same distance. However, ethanol still lags behind conventional fuel oil in terms of energy content, requiring about 50% more fuel by weight to achieve the same energy output.

The commercial use of ethanol is expected to expand from next year onward, given its abundant availability and low cost, which make it more attractive than other low-carbon fuels.

From a global perspective, ethanol is already used blended with gasoline and serves as a strategic alternative given the hostilities in the Middle East and the uncertainties surrounding oil prices. Not only does it offer shipowners another way to reduce their use of conventional fuels, but it also helps meet emissions reduction targets.

In this regard, one of Maersk's spokespersons stated that “It is worth exploring ethanol because it has an established global market and existing infrastructure, and it shares similar properties with methanol.”

Petrobras Acquires 50% of Itaimbezinho

The Itaimbezinho exploration block is an offshore area in the pre-salt region of Campos Basin, approximately 190 km off the coast of Rio de Janeiro.

On June 10, Petrobras announced its partnership with Equinor Brasil Energia Ltda. (Equinor) to acquire a 50% share in the block, which was previously wholly owned by the private company.

Once the transaction is completed, the consortium will consist of Equinor (50%) and Petrobras (50%). In this context, Pré-Sal Petróleo S.A. (PPSA) will act as the operator of the Production Sharing Agreement.

The deal is still subject to regulatory approvals, but it is already considered a strategic move to expand the future productive potential for oil and natural gas in one of the most promising regions of the Brazilian offshore sector.

Digital Bidding

On June 10, during a meeting at the Special Secretary for the Investment Partnerships Program (SEPPI), a technical cooperation agreement was signed between ANTAQ and Banco do Brasil to modernize the bidding processes for concessions and public-private partnerships in the port sector, with a focus on digital and hybrid workflows that increase efficiency and transparency.

The partnership will strengthen the digital transformation of concession and public-private partnership processes, promoting greater efficiency, transparency, and innovation in the mechanisms related to the organization of public bidding.

By incorporating digital solutions into port concession procedures, the initiative seeks to streamline administrative stages and reinforce governance practices, contributing to a more modern and predictable environment for investors and market participants interested in Brazil's port infrastructure sector.
