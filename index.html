The 12% tax rate on oil exports remains unchanged

Due to the escalation of the war between the United States and Iran, the Executive Management Committee of the Foreign Trade Chamber (Gecex-Camex) decided to maintain the export tax rate on crude oil and bituminous minerals at 12%.

The conflict intensified concerns about a possible reduction in the global supply of the commodity and a rise in international prices.

The measure was announced on July 9 and is intended to be temporary; it applies for up to 60 days and will be reviewed after 30 days. The committee argued that keeping the tax rate unchanged serves to maintain domestic market supply and ensure a steady supply of raw materials for Brazilian refineries.

OPEC Raises Forecast of Brazil's Growth in the Oil Market for 2026

The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) has raised its growth estimate for Brazilian liquid fuel production in 2026 compared to their last report. According to the organization's assessment, in this year, the expansion of Brazilian output will continue to be powered by the increase in productivity at the Búzios, Mero, Marlim, Bacalhau, and Wahoo projects, as well as new facilities coming onto operation in the Albacora Leste cluster.

Despite this, the organization kept its projections intact for average production in 2026 and 2027 and lowered its projection for 2027. Regarding 2027, the organization continues to estimate growth in production at Búzios, Bacalhau, and Wahoo, along with the new plans for the Búzios field and the resources of the Pampo-Enchova cluster. Nevertheless, it warns that higher development costs and persistent inflation could reduce the economic attractiveness of offshore projects, delay final investment decisions, and slow the pace of supply expansion.

On the macroeconomic front, OPEC maintained its projections for Brazil’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth at 2.0% in 2026 and 2.2% in 2027, noting as a risk the possibility that the United States might impose 25% tariffs on Brazilian imports.

Petrobras in Sao Tome and Principe

Petrobras has completed the acquisition of a participation and the assumption of operations in Block 3, located within the offshore zone of Sao Tome and Principe, in Africa. As a result, the consortium is now composed of Petrobras (with a 75% stake), in partnership with Oranto (15%) and the National Petroleum Agency of Sao Tome and Principe (10%).

The closure of the transaction was announced on July 9 and aims to replenish oil and gas reserves through exploration of new frontiers, thereby diversifying the company’s exploration portfolio.
