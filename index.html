A New Way to Commercialize Gas in Brazil?

Last Thursday, July 30, a resolution was approved that restructures Brazil’s natural gas marketing policy. The National Energy Policy Council (CNPE) authorized PPSA (Pre-Salt Petroleum) to hold short- and long-term auctions, and PPSA will now sell the federal government’s natural gas directly on the open market, giving priority to industrial sectors.

The regulation amends CNPE Resolution No. 15, dated October 29, 2018, and establishes guidelines for government-owned gas to be offered on economic terms and at prices considered competitive. According to the Ministry of Mines and Energy, the new rules could reduce the cost of the fuel by more than 50%.

Despite the authorization for the auctions, the product’s competitiveness will also depend on the conditions for access to transmission pipelines and processing facilities operated by Petrobras. Access to this infrastructure has yet to be defined, which was also one of the main obstacles to holding the auctions in the past.

Codeba Regulation Suspended!

In a preliminary ruling, the National Waterway Transportation Agency (ANTAQ) ordered the immediate suspension of a regulation issued by the Bahia State Dock Authority (Codeba) regarding the inspection of vessels. According to the Agency, the company allegedly exceeded its authority and invaded the jurisdiction of the Brazilian Navy.

In ANTAQ’s view, Codeba exceeded its role as a port authority by addressing issues related to waterway traffic safety. The company reportedly imposed requirements regarding vessel equipment and crew, as well as participation in safety procedures, the regulation of which falls exclusively under the jurisdiction of the Navy.

The suspension was ordered after ANTAQ accepted a complaint filed by the National Center for Transatlantic Navigation (Centronave) and the Brazilian Association of Cabotage Shipowners (ABAC) against Codeba. The measure affects the ports of Salvador, Aratu-Candeias, and Ilhéus, all in the state of Bahia.

Due to the precautionary nature of the decision, Cedeba’s technical and legal arguments have not yet been reviewed by the regulatory agency. The company argues that the rule only deals with documentary audits of ballast water management (water taken on board by ships to ensure stability), and should not be confused with certification and ship inspections, which fall under the jurisdiction of the Maritime Authority.

Update on Oil Exploration Operations in the Foz do Amazonas Basin.

During the Sergipe Oil and Gas 2026 event on July 29, Petrobras’ Director of Exploration and Production, Sylvia Anjos, stated that the state-owned company is expected to reach the reservoir at the Morpho field in the first weeks of August.

Previously scheduled for July, the completion of drilling at the pioneer well in the Foz do Amazonas Basin had to be postponed after a drilling mud leak, in addition to a delay in Ibama’s authorization to resume drilling.

The company states that, given the great depth and the fact that this is an unknown area, work has been carried out with greater caution, which has led to speculations about possible difficulties in reaching the reservoir or even the absence of oil at the bottom of the well. Currently, the ODN II rig is 500 meters away from reaching its target.
